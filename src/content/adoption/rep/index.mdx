[A State Department memo](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/immigrant-visa-processing-updates-for-nationalities-at-high-risk-of-public-benefits-usage.html) issued January 14 pauses the issuance of all immigration visas for 75 countries. This pause applies to the visas that international adoptive parents use to bring their children home. We, and many other families, are impacted by the delay.

[Here is more on our situation.](/adoption/adoption-blocked)

We are requesting people contact their representatives about the unintended consequence the memo has on international adoptions.

Below is a template letter.

**How to send this letter:**

The easiest way to do this is to go to [**Democracy.io**](http://Democracy.io). It lets you message your Rep and both Senators at once. Just copy/paste the subject and body below, and replace **\[City/State\]** and the contact information at the bottom with your information. Don't worry if the bold formatting disappears when you paste it—the text is what matters.

*The letter below works for anyone—whether you know our family personally or just want to help fix this policy. Feel free to personalize it, but the template is ready to send as-is.*

---

**Subject: Please Exempt Adoption Visas from the January 14 Visa Pause**

I am writing as your constituent in **\[City/State\]** about a State Department policy that is inadvertently keeping children separated from their American families.

**The Human Cost**

American families who have completed every legal requirement to adopt children from abroad are now blocked from bringing their children home.

One example: a military family in Georgia—a veteran who served in Iraq and Afghanistan and his wife, an engineer—has spent over a year completing the Hague Convention process to adopt two teenagers from Colombia. Both governments have approved them. They were weeks away from traveling to finalize the adoption when this memo paused their children's visas.

The teenagers are still in foster care in Colombia. Under Colombian law, they cannot be told about the adoption until final court approval. They don't know a family is waiting for them.

This family is not alone. The January 14 memo affects adoptions from 75 countries.

**The Policy Problem**

The memo pauses immigrant visas citing "public charge" concerns—the risk that immigrants will rely on public benefits.

This rationale cannot legally apply to children on IH-3 adoption visas:

**1. Adoptive families already prove financial capacity** to USCIS before being approved to adopt.

**2. These children become U.S. citizens upon entry.** Under the Child Citizenship Act, IH-3 children automatically acquire citizenship the moment they arrive. The public charge statute applies only to aliens—not citizens. It is legally impossible for these children to become public charges.

This appears to be an unintended consequence.

**What I'm Asking**

The pause took effect **January 21, 2026**. I respectfully ask you to:

**1. Submit a congressional inquiry** to the State Department requesting clarification on whether IH-3 adoption visas will be exempted.

**2. Sign or initiate a bipartisan letter** to Secretary Rubio requesting an exemption for intercountry adoption visas.

**3. Contact the White House** to flag this unintended consequence before the effective date.

These families followed every law. The children will be citizens upon arrival. Please help bring them home.
