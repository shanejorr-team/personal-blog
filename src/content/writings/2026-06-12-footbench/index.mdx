### TL;DR: Footbench explained

Footbench is a quasi-LLM evaluation that places LLMs in the coach's box. Models are asked
to provide underutilized strategic football recommendations and then told to gauge their confidence
in their answers. Other LLMs, as judges, rate the responses by pairing models against each other,
two at a time, and picking a winner.

The 12 models (coaches) are:

| Family | Models |
|---|---|
| Anthropic (Claude) | Fable 5, Opus 4.8, Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5 |
| OpenAI (GPT) | GPT-5.5 Pro, GPT-5.5, GPT-5.4 mini |
| Google (Gemini) | Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview, Gemini 3.5 Flash, Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite |
| DeepSeek | DeepSeek V4 Pro, DeepSeek V4 Flash |

### What is evaluated

The evaluation measures three competencies:

#### 1. Reasoning with evidence (the "analytics" row)**

Underutilized NFL strategies that must be **backed by public football analytics** — win-probability
models, expected-points research, published findings. The model is asked to surface ideas
that the data supports but that real coaches still underuse (the classic example being
"go for it on 4th down more often"). This rewards models that actually know the football
analytics literature and can reason from it.

#### 2. Reasoning on intuition (the "intuition" row)

These strategies must come from the coach's **own judgment in areas where public analytics is
absent, silent, or mixed**. The model has to briefly justify *why* the idea qualifies as
intuition rather than settled analytics and it is explicitly told **not** to just restate
the analytics consensus in different words. This tests whether a model can form an original,
defensible opinion when the data won't hand it the answer.

#### 3. Measuring confidence in its beliefs (the "priors")

For each recommended strategy, the model must quantify its uncertainty as a **Bayesian
prior** over a single, precisely defined quantity:

> the change in the team's **expected single-game scoring margin** (your points minus the
> opponent's), in **points**, caused by adopting the strategy versus not. Positive means
> it helps; negative means it backfires.

To express that belief the model must provide:

- a **most-plausible value** (its single best guess, and it's told not to default to round
  numbers);
- a **95% interval** — the low/high range it's ~95% sure contains the true effect;
- the **shape** of its belief (symmetric, or skewed toward the upside or downside) and how
  heavy the tails are (how much chance it gives to a surprisingly large effect);
- a matching **probability distribution family** — `normal`, `student_t`, or `skew_normal` —
  with the actual parameters, and the math showing how it converted its plain-English belief
  into those parameters.

Models recommend three strategies in both the **analytics** and **intuition** strategy buckets.

1. **Game management** — clock and timeout use, challenges, 4th-down and 2-point decisions, and
  similar in-game choices.
2. **Offense** — play-calling, scheme, personnel groupings, tempo.
3. **Defense** — play-calling, scheme, coverage, personnel.

Models then state their confidence in each of their 6 recommended strategies (2 compentencies x 3 buckets)
by assignming Bayesian priors.

### How responses are evaluated

We use pairwise evaluation to calcualte model scores with **four LLMs acting as juges**:

- OpenAI GPT 5.5;
- Claude Opus 4.8;
- Google Gemini Flash 3.5;
- DeepSeek V4 Pro.

In pairwise evaluation two coach models (candidate models in AI speak) compete head-to-head
through the following process:

- All four judges individually choose which recommendations are best between the two coach models.
The judges do not know which model created the responses.
- The judges pick a winner in two categories:
    1. **Soundness of recommendations**
        - Better NFL strategy recommendation: underutilized, credibly win-positive, football-literate
        rationales, correct analytics-vs-intuition bucket, distinct picks in the right grid cells.
    2. **Reasonableness of Bayesian priors**
        - Better prior choices: distribution family matches shape/tails/support, plausible
        football effect sizes, reasonable 95% intervals, correct conversions, honest uncertainty
        for weak evidence cells.
- Judges are told to judge each criterion independently, so the same answer needn't win both.
- After judges pick a winner once, the order of the responses from the two coaches is reversed
and the judges pick winners again. This controls for position bias.

This process is ran for every combination of coach models, leading to 1,056 comparisons
(66 pairs x 2 categories x 2 orderings x 4 judges).

The coach model's final displayed score is win percentage: wins / comparisons * 100, overall or by criterion.

### Limitations of footbench

A critique of the NFL combine is that the evaluations are too quantitative and do not measure what matters
in an actual NFL game. After all, you are not running straight and uninterrupted for 40
yards or bench pressing in a game. "Just watch the game tape", old-timers exclaim.

Of course, we are full of baises and no two players play in the same environment:
same team, competetion, schemas, etc. This makes player comparisons from game tape difficult.
Especially small differences, such as identifying the best quarterback and third best
quarterback. A difference worth millions of dollars.

Footbench suffers from the opposite problem. The evaluation throws subjective questions
at the coach models; questions lacking a 'right answer'. This places them in game-like environments.
The cost, however, is that it is hard to know what a 'right answer' looks like.

There is no ground-truth to determine which coach in a pairwise comparison had the better
answer. The judge evaluators just watch the game tape and decide based on the eyeball test.

Another problem is that footbench is circular. LLM models are judging LLM models in subjective
criteria. If llm models, in general, have blindspots or weaknesses in evaluating NFL strategies
then the judges will have these blindspots and weaknesses as well. Depending on the depth of
the blind spots, the blind could be leading the blind.

We try to smooth over these weaknesses by having four models act as judges. But, we do not
fool ourselves into thinking they are eliminated.

Ideally, the NFL's greatest minds would act as judges and rate the models. Absent that,
LLMs will have to do.

Given the weaknesses of the evaluation process, there is value in reading the responses
and comparing how different models respond and how they judge their own confidence. As a
plus, you can learn football strategy by reading.
