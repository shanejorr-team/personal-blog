I rejoice with the Venezuelan people for being freed from their tyrant's foot. Maduro was a vicious dictator who attacked his own people and, along with Chavez, drove the Venezuelan economy into the ground. Good riddance.

Venezuelans have hope. Hope for their kids, their future, and themselves. Rejoice in this hope.

The country and people deserve a government that will govern for Venezuelans. A government where the economic spoils of their resources go to Venezuelans, where their dignity and rights are preserved, and where they have a voice over the forces that control them.

Trump claims that the United States will govern Venezuela. By United States, he means him and his inner circle. Will he govern with the interests of Venezuelans as his priority?

I'm worried.

He has said that the governance and rebuilding will not cost the United States anything because the United States will be reimbursed through oil revenue. After US oil companies and the US government get their cut, what is left over for Venezuelans?[^1]

I'm worried.

<InlineFigure src={photo1} alt="United States exploitation of Venezuela's resources" caption="Venezuela's resources must benefit Venezuelans, not just American business interests." />

Do we think a man, Trump, who flouts democracy in his own country and attempted to stay in power after losing an election - like Maduro successfully did last year - will work for democracy in Venezuela?

I'm worried.

Do we think a man who has no patience for human rights when they hinder his cruel policies will ensure the new Venezuelan government protects the human rights of Venezuelans?

I'm worried.

What about when instilling democracy and protecting human rights obstructs oil and cash flows to American business interests?

I'm worried.

Throughout the 20th century, the United States, its citizens and governments, showed little concern for the people of Latin America during interventions. This held even with presidents who were saints compared to Trump. Will this time be different?

I'm worried.

Look at how Trump has governed his own people. Now give him unconstrained power, without constitutional and democratic checks.

I'm worried.

I'm reminded of King Leopold II, who ruled Belgium from 1865 to 1909. At home, he was a respected and benevolent ruler. In his personal fiefdom of the Congo, however, he ruled with ruthless and oppressive precision.

Here are [Claude's reasons](https://claude.ai/share/828b88c4-172b-4cb7-bbee-582f4b86c7d9) for Leopold's contrasting sides:

- Legal Structure: At home, Leopold operated under constitutional constraints, a parliament, and press scrutiny. The Congo had none of these.[^2]
- Distance and invisibility: Few Europeans witnessed or heard about Leopold's brutality in the Congo.[^3]
- Economic Incentive: The rubber boom made exploitation profitable to Leopold.[^4]
- Racism: European ideology at the time, the late 1800s and early 1900s, viewed Africans as subhuman, making atrocities easier to rationalize.[^5]

This doesn't help my worry.

No doubt, Leopold is an extreme example. But reality does not have to be this extreme to still cause worry. Even a "30% Leopold" is unacceptable. Plus, Trump isn't even considered a benevolent ruler in his own country.

Thus, I'm worried.

Venezuela's future is uncertain. But there is one certainty: Trump will not govern Venezuela for the benefit of Venezuelans. He has already said as much and we should take him at his word.

This is where we come in. And my 'we' is concrete: voters in the United States like you and me.

Despite news to the contrary, democracy is not dead in the United States. The proof? Name a single election over the past 10 years where the rightful winner did not take their seat on time?

**For good or ill, we, American voters, are now the voice of the Venezuelan people**. We are their only voice.

We, you and me, must advocate for the Venezuelan people.

We, you and me, must vote with them in mind.

<InlineFigure src={photo2} alt="Voter in a voting booth." caption="American voters must vote with Venezuela in mind." />

We, you and me, must give a damn about them. We must because Trump won't.

We, you and me, must demand that the United States governs Venezuela in the interest of Venezuelans and not Americans. Saying "Well, they are better off than under Maduro" is unacceptable. Our moral standards must be higher.

We must demand nothing short of a Venezuela First approach to governing Venezuela. Anything less is a moral failure for us all, you and me.

Our votes matter. Our voices matter. Venezuelans are relying on us.

Are we up to the task? Mid-terms are in a year. I worry we are not.
